# Undercut — Rules (v1)

## Objective

At the end of the game, each player reveals their 3 cards. Whoever's 3 cards add up to the **lowest total** wins the round.

## Components

- One standard 52-card deck (no jokers). For 5+ players, consider two decks shuffled together so the draw pile doesn't run dry too fast.

## Setup

1. Shuffle the deck.
2. Deal 3 cards face-down to each player.
3. Each player privately looks at their own 3 cards **once**, then returns them face-down in the same spots. From this point on, you're playing from memory — you don't get to re-check your own cards except by using a King.
4. The remaining deck is placed face-down in the center as the **Draw Pile**.

## Card Values

| Card | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A | 1 | Ace is low |
| 2–6, 8, 9, 10 | Face value | Plain cards, no special power |
| 7 | 7 | Action card: **Scramble** |
| J | 11 | Action card: **Blind Trade** |
| Q | 12 | Action card: **Peek Opponent** |
| K | 13 | Action card: **Peek Own** |

Action cards count at these face values whenever they're still sitting in someone's hand at game end — played or not, used or not.

## Turn Sequence

On your turn, draw the top card of the Draw Pile and look at it. Play then passes clockwise.

**Drew a plain card (A, 2–6, 8, 9, 10):**
Compare it against your memory of your 3 cards.
- If it's lower than at least one of them, you *may* swap it into that slot. This is always your choice — even a qualifying lower card can simply be discarded if you'd rather leave your hand alone.
- If you swap it into a slot that currently holds an **unplayed** action card, that action card's power triggers as it's discarded (see below) — your one chance to cash it in.
- If you don't swap — because nothing qualifies, or just by choice — the drawn card goes straight to the dead discard pile and your turn ends.

**Drew an action card (7, J, Q, K):**
Choose one:
- **Play it immediately** — resolve its power right now, then discard it. It never enters your hand.
- **Bank it** — swap it into any one of your 3 slots, ignoring the "must be lower" rule entirely; its utility is worth the value hit. The card it replaces is discarded as normal — **if the replaced card is itself an unplayed action card, its power triggers as it's discarded**, same as any other action-card departure. Its own power sits unused for now.

**Cashing in a card you're holding (banked, or one you started with):**
The *only* way to trigger the power of an action card already in your hand is via a swap that removes it from your slot — either a qualifying lower plain-card swap, or a bank-over-bank swap (a fresh action card banked into that slot). There's no separate "activate on your turn" move. If the game ends while you're still holding one unused, it just counts at face value.

## Action Cards

- **King — Peek Own:** Look at all 3 of your own cards, then return them face-down.
- **Queen — Peek Opponent:** Look at all 3 cards of any chosen opponent, then return them face-down.
- **Jack — Blind Trade:** The player who plays the Jack chooses both the opponent and the exact card position to target, and their own card position to send — then the two cards swap without either player looking at either value.
- **Seven — Scramble:** Pick one opponent and rearrange the physical order of their 3 face-down cards without looking at them. Pure sabotage — it invalidates anything they'd memorized about those positions.

## Ending the Game & Scoring

The round ends the instant the Draw Pile is exhausted. All players reveal their 3 cards and total the values — lowest total wins. For a longer match, everyone else could add their total to a running score across rounds, with a target score (e.g. 100) ending the match.
